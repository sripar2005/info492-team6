Round 1 · Before Demo 3

Mid-quarter interview · owner’s rep for a school district

What they said
  • Contract rules can be checked: markups “are defined. If they’re not defined in the contract, there’s a problem.”
  • People still decide; the agent’s output feeds his review.
  • “Garbage in, garbage out”: inputs decide everything.
  • Pricing math is already solved by his tool; the costly disputes are delays, site conditions and permitting.
What we changed
  • Demo 3 followed his review steps: cover sheet, backup tie-out, markups against contract limits, scope match.
  • A “needs a person” route for delay, site conditions and permitting.
  • Our edge moved earlier and contract-specific: every flag quotes the clause behind it.
Round 2 · Between Demo 3 and Demo 4

Class visit · CM from a large general contractor

What they said
  • Firms have data-sharing concerns about sending contracts and project records to cloud AI.
What we changed
  • The AI runs on the user’s own device (free local models through Ollama). No API keys, nothing sent to a cloud provider.
  • Every AI step logs a receipt: model, citations, and who accepted it.
Round 3 · Before Demo 4 (recorded with permission)

Demo 3 prototype review · owner’s rep for a school district

What they said
  • Liked the two-step check (confirm the numbers, then check), and the whole team “literally working off the same pages.”
  • “I’d much rather have a not return something with comments than return it.”
  • Next layer of value: schedule. Is the change on the critical path? Use a baseline schedule and its updates.
  • Work out what a day of delay costs from the schedule of values and general conditions.
  • Review delays by the architect or owner are a real source of claims.
  • Asked about AI safety standards and certifications, and a direct Procore link.
  • Pointed us to public records for real data: public contracting documents can be requested.
What we changed
  • Demo 4 delay check: time impact against the latest schedule update not already including the change, critical path only.
  • Cost per day from the contract’s stated rate, shown next to the schedule-of-values general-conditions spread.
  • Baseline schedule plus updates; review clocks from the contract (architect 14 days, RFI 10 days).
  • The platform: one record from sub to owner, visibility following the contract chain.
  • Kept the two-step check, and made it required before a proposal can be sent.
  • Procore: copy-paste today; a connector is planned (not built).
  • Real data: public RFI logs and contracts from state agencies, used for the Demo 4 evaluation.
