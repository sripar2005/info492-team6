Then the whole chain works from one record

Sub, GC/CM, architect and owner’s rep usually re-check each proposal from their own copies. ClauseStream keeps one project record (RFIs, ASIs, submittals, daily reports, approved minutes, contract and specs) and one thread per change. Each finding links to its source, and a return has to say why.

How it works

Done by a person

ClauseStream finds the RFI, field report or ASI behind it, drafts the formal proposal citing each one, and checks markups, fee, notice and days against the contract.

AI drafts, code checks every number
3Every party reviews the same thread

GC/CM, architect and owner’s rep see one thread with its evidence trail. A return needs a reason, so the next version fixes the right thing.

Done by a person

What it checks today

Computed by code from your contract, each finding cited to its clause.

Money
  • Subcontractor markups by tier
  • CM fee and what it may be charged on
  • Labor burden and equipment rates
  • Totals and the approval limit
Notice
  • Deadline by cause, from the contract
  • Weekends and holidays pushed to the next business day
  • Late notice flagged with the clause
Time
  • Is the change on the critical path?
  • Days it really adds, from your own schedule
  • What the contract pays for those days

For owners and their reps

Proposals arrive already checked against your contract, with every claim tied to the RFI, report or clause it rests on. Time claims come with the schedule analysis behind them. You decide; the trail is there when someone asks why.

For GC/CMs and subs

See what would bounce before you send it: the markup over the limit, the fee on excluded items, the notice that’s late. Fix it once instead of after a round trip.
