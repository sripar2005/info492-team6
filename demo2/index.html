Is this document a clarification, or a change we must give notice on?

A contractor that misses a change, or gives notice late, can lose the right to be paid for it. We gave Claude 30 documents a general contractor receives and asked it to classify each one, cite the clause, and name the notice deadline.

What changed since Demo 1

Iteration
Demo 1 found

Claude extracted 94.4% of the contract's core rules, but rules that say "give notice under Section 6.2(a)" lost the 7-day deadline that 6.2(a) sets. A checker built on that would miss the clock.

Demo 2 tests

Every possible change must be traced to one Section 6.2 rule, often through a cross-reference: an ASI goes through 6.8, a submittal comment through 8.3, and meeting minutes don't count as notice under 7.7.

New design

The AI picks the rule; code computes the date, including the contract's weekend and holiday roll-forward. We score the AI's own date and the code date side by side.

Data, limits, and files

Read before citing

The 30 documents

  • Synthetic, written against Demo 1's contract (modeled on Seattle's Webster School GC/CM project).
  • 15 clarifications, 15 possible changes; all four 6.2 rules appear.
  • 11 tricky cases, such as a "no-cost" ASI that adds work and an owner email that says "don't proceed."
  • Notice dates are computed by the generation script, not typed by hand.

Limits

  • The documents, labels, and contract share one author; no teammate has independently labeled them yet.
  • 30 short, clean documents on one contract; real ones are messier.
  • A re-run may differ slightly; the committed results are the record.
